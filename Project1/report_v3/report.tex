\begin{abstract}

This project studies the numerical integration of the polar-factor
gradient flow
\[
\dot{\mathbf{X}}
=
\mathbf{X}
\left(
\mathbf{I}_n-\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{X}
\right),
\]
which drives a nonsingular matrix toward the orthogonal factor of its
polar decomposition. Three numerical methods are investigated:
Explicit Euler, classical fourth-order Runge--Kutta (RK4), and Implicit
Euler solved by a damped Newton iteration.

The numerical solutions are validated against the exact finite-time SVD
solution and an independent tight-tolerance Radau reference. The
experiments examine convergence order, absolute stability, adaptive
step-size control, computational work, Lyapunov decay, singular-value
evolution, orthogonality defect, and rank-deficient behaviour. The
measured convergence slopes are approximately $1.010$, $3.927$, and
$1.014$ for Explicit Euler, RK4, and Implicit Euler, respectively, in
agreement with their theoretical orders. The exact SVD solution and the
Radau reference differ by only $1.16\times10^{-13}$ at the final time.

The results show that the explicit methods are subject to restrictive
local stability limits during the rapid initial transient, whereas
Implicit Euler remains more robust for coarse step sizes at the cost of
nonlinear solves. Adaptive step-size control reduces the step size in
the fast transient region. Under matched terminal accuracy, RK4 requires
the fewest right-hand-side evaluations among the tested configurations.
The rank-deficient experiment also confirms that a zero singular mode is
preserved and the solution approaches a partial isometry rather than an
orthogonal matrix.

\end{abstract}
